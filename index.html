SheetHound — the spreadsheet watchdog

Flagship

The problem. Before a workbook goes to a client or an auditor, nobody has time to check what is still inside it. Broken external links that point at someone else's file. Cascading #REF! errors from a single deleted row. Sheets that were hidden rather than deleted. Numbers that quietly stopped agreeing with the source. You find these out after you send it.

The fix. SheetHound opens the workbook in the browser and runs a pre-flight forensic pass before anything leaves your desk — lineage on every formula, external links traced, hidden XML sheets unmasked, and typography hygiene flagged. It never uploads the file, because the file is the client's confidential information.

Where I stand

Pro-AI. Still sceptical.

Five pieces I have written this year. They are the argument I actually hold, not a commentary on somebody else's — grouped by whether they are about AI in a finance function or about working beside it.

I use AI every working day and I would not want to go back to not using it. It has made me materially faster at the parts of accounting that are typing, formatting, reconciling and checking — and it has also made it very easy to be confidently wrong, which in this profession is expensive.

So the line is narrow and I hold it firmly. AI takes the problem, never the confidential data, and never the sign-off. Double-entry, cut-off, intercompany, and the judgement that a number is wrong before anyone can prove it — human, every time. The nine tools above exist because of that line rather than in spite of it.
